# Writing the letter

How `cover-letter.md` is composed. Reached from `skills/cover-letter/SKILL.md` step 2.

Inputs: `posting.md` `## Brief`, `## Posting` and `## Match`, the tailored resume in `tailored-resume/`, `profile.md` `## Letter rules` and `## Metrics bank`.

Sentence-level rules — length, openings, banned turns of phrase, tense of a finished role, the line-by-line review — live in `shared/references/writing-rules.md` and apply to every line written here.

## Proof map

Written first, above the letter. Take from `## Match` the requirements this letter will answer, one line each, in the same shape:

```
requirement → evidence → source → quality (direct | analogous | transferable)
```

Keep the lines the letter actually uses, plus every must-have of the posting. A must-have with no evidence, not even transferable, is written with its hole named: the application goes to `to validate` and the letter claims nothing in its place.

## Letter

Language of the posting. 150 to 250 words across the three paragraphs, unless `preferences.md` `## Application rules` sets another band.

The shape is the French one, whatever the language: a subject line, a salutation, three paragraphs a French recruiter reads as "Vous, Je, Nous", a sign-off. `## Letter` carries them in that order. It is what a text field receives, and what the template's placeholders receive when the form wants a file.

1. **Subject line** — `Objet : Lettre de motivation pour le poste de <role>`, the role as the posting titles it. In English, `Subject: Cover letter for the <role> position`.
2. **Salutation** — the addressee by name, looked for before settling on the generic form. The `Contact:` line of `posting.md` first; empty, open the posting URL once and read the recruiter card, the "meet the team" or contact block, the signature of the contact e-mail; a name found goes into `Contact:`. Named: `Madame Dupont,` or `Monsieur Dupont,`; in English, `Dear Jane Dupont,`. Nothing on the posting: `Madame, Monsieur,`, in English `Dear Hiring Manager,`. No search beyond the posting.
3. **Vous** — the company and the role, two to four sentences: what the company does, named concretely, what this role is for, and what caught the candidate's eye. The keywords of `## Brief` appear here as the posting writes them. The candidate stays out of this paragraph.
4. **Je** — the candidate, the longest paragraph: two or three quantified results that answer the responsibilities of `## Brief`. Each result names the role that produced it, with the figures of `## Metrics bank`. They are the same evidence the tailored resume carries, told as short stories rather than bullets, in the candidate's own voice per `## Letter rules`.
5. **Nous** — what the candidate would do in this role, for this company: the responsibilities of the posting taken up as a proposal, two or three sentences, closed by the invitation to talk. Availability, notice and location belong to the form fields, not here.
6. **Sign-off** — one closing formula, `Cordialement,`, then the candidate's name.

The candidate's own narrative rules — what to say first, what to keep to a single sentence, how a company or a client is named — are in `profile.md` `## Letter rules` and override the shape above.

## Before returning

- Word count inside the band.
- Every sentence reviewed line by line per `shared/references/writing-rules.md`.
- Each named company carries only the results filed under its own role and dates.
- Chronology checked against the resume before any "earlier" or "later".
- Each proof answers a requirement that appears in the proof map.
- "Vous" names the company and the role, "Je" carries the results, "Nous" the proposal: none of the three does the work of another.
